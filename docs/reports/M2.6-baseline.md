## What this is

The simulated baby was run for 7 days, 20 times per combination, for every age band × temperament (20 combinations), with two simulated caregivers:

- **Attentive** (`M2.6-attentive-7d.md`): a parent nearby. Hears fussing and responds within minutes, in a sensible order: check diaper, feed if it has been a while, burp, play with an older baby, rock. Use this report for **how the baby behaves** when well cared for: sleep, crying, feeding, diapers.
- **Away** (`M2.6-delayed-7d.md`): responds only when the app alerts, 10–20 minutes later. Use this report for **how often the app alerts** (care requests), because in real use the person is not watching the baby every minute.

Each metric is compared with its target from `docs/06`. **OUT** marks a miss, and *Levers* names the parameters that move it. Nothing has been tuned yet: this baseline shows where the current values land, so we can tune together.

## Findings and recommendations (the reviewer decides)

These come from the reports plus a diagnostic that tallied which hidden need was behind each minute of fussing. Each recommendation changes a `DESIGN_ASSUMPTION` value. None has been applied.

1. **Babies calm too slowly once a need is met.** After a feed or a diaper change, distress fades over about 14 minutes (`model.distressFallPerMinute` 0.04). This "fussy tail" is the largest single source of extra crying. *Proposal:* 0.10 per minute (about 5–6 minutes). *Question:* how quickly does a baby whose need was met usually settle?
2. **Feeding swallows air too fast.** Gas reaches the discomfort level about 8 minutes into every feed (`model.gasPerFeedMinute` 0.03 against `gasDiscomfortAt` 0.25), so many feeds end in gassy crying. *Proposal:* 0.015 per minute, so gas matters on longer feeds or when burping is skipped.
3. **Older babies get overstimulated by ordinary play.** Stimulation wears off over 2+ hours (`model.stimulationDecayPerMinute` 0.005), so normal play sessions add up. *Proposal:* 0.02 per minute (about 35 minutes to wear off).
4. **Too much sleep from 4 months.** Total sleep is about 16.8 h against 12–16 h, with one nap too many. Sleep bouts and wake windows (`wakeWindowMinutes`) need adjusting together. *Question:* typical wake windows at 4–6, 7–9 and 10–12 months?
5. **Too many night wakings in every band.** *Levers:* `model.nightHungerRateScale` (night hunger slows by 30% today), `circadianStrength`, `selfSettleProbNight`. *Question:* how often should a well-fed 4–6 / 7–9 / 10–12-month-old fully wake at night?
6. **Too few happy sounds.** An awake, content baby goes 25–45 minutes without a sound against a 12–20 minute target. *Proposal:* roughly double `vocalRatePerAwakeHour` in every band.
7. **Wet diapers run high for newborns.** About 11 per day against 6–10, because the "more likely after a feed" boost (`model.postFeedVoidMultiplier` 2.0) adds on top of the daily rate. *Proposal:* lower the boost to 1.5, or lower `wetDiapersPerDay` to compensate.
8. **Feeds come late for 4–6 and 10–12 months.** Partly the age ranges, partly the caregiver waiting until a feed is due. *Question:* confirm the feed intervals in the spreadsheet.

## For the owner (not behavior realism)

- **Auto-pause edge case.** With the away caregiver, a few babies (mostly 7–12 months, one 2–3 month run) auto-paused even though no request went unanswered for long. Their alerts calmed down on their own before the caregiver arrived, and the 4-hour clock kept counting from the first one (`docs/01` B1). *Options:* (a) start the 4-hour clock only from a request that expires; (b) reset it whenever a request calms down by itself; (c) keep as is. Recommendation: (b).
- **Day-to-day variety.** Consecutive days share almost no care-request times (similarity 0.00–0.06 against a limit of 0.6). Schedules are very varied, as the brief asked. If they feel too random in testing, `rhythmicitySigmaScale` makes them steadier.
