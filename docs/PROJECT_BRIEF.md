# Project Brief: Realistic Baby & Toddler Care Simulator

**Project Status:** Initial planning and product design  
**Target Platform:** Android first, iOS after proven sales  
**Product Type:** Premium mobile companion app for reborn baby dolls and other realistic dolls  
**Design Direction:** Modern, polished, realistic, immersive — NOT a cartoon or traditional game  
**Business Model:** One-time app purchase with optional paid expansions

---

## 1. Project Overview

We want to develop a realistic baby and toddler care simulation app for people who own reborn dolls or other realistic baby dolls.

The app will act as the baby's behavior and care system while the user physically interacts with their doll.

The primary reference is **BabySitMe**, an existing Android application.

Reference application:  
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.babysitme.babysitme

BabySitMe is a good starting concept, but its interactions are too limited and often too infrequent. During actual use, the app can go three to four hours without requiring any interaction.

We want a much more immersive experience that reflects how real babies behave throughout the day and night.

The simulation should be inspired by the electronic infant simulators used in high school parenting classes, particularly Realityworks RealCare Baby.

The project will benefit from my wife's years of childcare training and experience. She will help establish realistic behaviors, developmental expectations, care requirements, and appropriate responses.

### Core Vision

**Make caring for a reborn doll feel more like caring for a real baby or toddler, without requiring special electronic hardware or turning the experience into a video game.**

The app must be simple to operate but sophisticated in its underlying behavior.

## 2. Target Audience

Primary users:

- Reborn baby doll collectors and enthusiasts
- People who enjoy realistic baby doll roleplay
- Doll owners who want more interactive care experiences
- Adults who want realistic infant simulation

Secondary potential users:

- People interested in learning infant care
- Childcare students
- Expecting parents interested in educational simulations

The initial product should focus on the reborn doll community rather than trying to become a professional medical or educational training application.

The app should work with any physical doll. No sensors, Bluetooth doll hardware, or special accessories should be required.

## 3. Competitor Research

### Primary Competitor: BabySitMe

Google Play:  
https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.babysitme.babysitme

At the time of research, BabySitMe was listed at $3.99, with 5,000+ downloads and approximately 3.9 stars.

Existing features include:

- Baby naming
- Feeding
- Burping
- Diaper changes
- Rocking
- Crying notifications
- Easy, Medium, Hard, and Play modes
- 24-hour simulation schedules
- Start and stop simulation

### Weaknesses to Address

1. **Long inactive periods:** Users can experience three or four hours without interaction. We want more natural awake periods, vocalizations, care events, and activity.
2. **Limited age customization:** Users should choose the developmental age represented by their doll.
3. **Limited behavior:** Add more care needs, realistic responses, and age-specific activities.
4. **Limited personalization:** Different temperaments should produce different behaviors.
5. **Repetitive interactions:** Care should be influenced by previous events rather than generated by an isolated random timer.
6. **Multiple babies:** Users have requested the ability to simulate more than one baby.
7. **Audio and notifications:** Improve crying alerts, soothing sounds, sleeping sounds, and reliability.
8. **Android compatibility:** The competitor's listing warns of crashes associated with Android updates. Our app should prioritize current Android compatibility and background reliability.
9. **Modern interface:** Provide a cleaner, more professional interface that feels like a realistic caregiving companion.

These are product opportunities, not claims that every competitor user experiences every problem. Validate specific complaints through reviews and beta testing.

We should study the general frequency, duration, variability, and distribution of care requirements.

However, our application should use an **original behavior engine**, not duplicate Realityworks' proprietary schedules, sounds, software, or branding.

Our model should be developed using publicly available infant development guidance, original behavior rules, and review by someone experienced in childcare.

Additional research sources:

- CDC Developmental Milestones: https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/

Use age-appropriate guidance, recognizing that actual infant behavior varies considerably.

## 5. Baby Age Selection

The user must be able to select the age of the baby to match their physical doll.

Initial age profiles:

| Age | Developmental focus |
|---|---|
| Newborn, 0–4 weeks | Frequent feeding, irregular sleep, crying, basic comfort |
| 2–3 months | Coos, social interaction, emerging routines |
| 4–6 months | Laughing, reaching, play, changing sleep patterns |
| 7–9 months | Babbling, interactive play, possible separation anxiety |
| 10–12 months | Gestures, exploration, increasing independence |
| Toddler, 13–24 months | Walking-related activity, communication, emotional expression, independence |

**Important:** Toddler support is part of the product vision, but should be implemented after the initial infant simulation is reliable. Do not expand the MVP unnecessarily.

Users should be able to keep a baby at a selected age indefinitely. Automatic aging is not required.

Age must influence:

- Feeding needs and intervals
- Sleep duration and patterns
- Awake periods
- Crying and fussing
- Soothing requirements
- Play and social interaction
- Developmentally appropriate vocalizations
- Diapering
- Activity levels

Age-specific ranges should be researched and reviewed rather than invented.

---

## 6. Baby Temperament

Age and temperament must be independent settings.

Four initial temperament profiles:

**Easygoing**
- Generally settles more easily
- More predictable routines
- Longer content periods
- Less frequent additional soothing

**Average**
- Typical variation in needs
- Moderate fussiness
- Changing sleep and feeding patterns
- Normal variation in soothing requirements

**Demanding**
- More frequent caregiver attention
- Shorter settled periods
- More persistent fussiness
- More complex soothing

**Highly Sensitive**
- Greater sensitivity to stimulation
- May need more time to settle
- More reactive to transitions
- Requires careful, responsive comforting

These are simulation profiles, not medical diagnoses.

Optional conditions such as colic-like crying or reflux-related discomfort may be considered later, but must be carefully researched and should not be treated simply as higher difficulty.

## 7. Core Behavior Simulation Engine

**This is the most important component of the application.**

The simulation must not consist of a fixed list of care notifications or simple timers.

It should use a persistent, state-based behavior engine with controlled randomness.

### Internal Baby States

At minimum, track:

- Hunger
- Fatigue
- Sleep state
- Diaper status
- Comfort
- Stimulation
- Mood or fussiness
- Time since feeding
- Time since diaper change
- Time since sleeping
- Time since successful soothing
- Recent caregiver actions

The baby's internal state should influence which events occur, when they occur, and how the baby responds.

### Randomness Requirements

We want meaningful variation rather than arbitrary alarms.

Examples:

- Feeding intervals vary within age-appropriate ranges.
- A baby may wake earlier or later than expected.
- Diaper events are not always tied to feeding.
- A baby may become fussy without being hungry.
- A baby may need comforting after feeding.
- Some soothing attempts take longer than others.
- A baby may be awake and content.
- The same baby should not follow an identical schedule every day.

### Event Frequency

One of our major objectives is to improve the long periods of inactivity experienced with BabySitMe.

The app should have more natural activity throughout the day, including:

- Care requests
- Awake periods
- Content vocalizations
- Fussing
- Play
- Sleep transitions
- Settling interactions

However, do not force unnecessary care events merely to keep the user busy. A long sleep period can be realistic for some ages and circumstances.

Differentiate between **audible activity** and **care requiring immediate attention**.

Use realistic age-dependent boundaries and test 24-hour simulations to avoid excessive or insufficient activity.

### Example Scenario

A three-month-old baby begins crying.

The user tries feeding, but the baby was fed recently and does not appear hungry.

The user checks the diaper, which is dry.

The user chooses to comfort and rock the baby.

After a variable settling period, the baby calms down.

The simulator updates the baby's comfort and fatigue states.

Later behavior should reflect the previous interaction.

The user should not always know the exact reason for crying before trying to provide care.

## 8. Care Activities

Initial core activities:

1. Feeding
2. Burping
3. Diaper changing
4. Rocking
5. Holding and comforting
6. Putting baby to sleep
7. Waking and sleep transitions
8. Play and social interaction

Additional activities for later stages:

- Tummy time while awake and supervised
- Talking to baby
- Age-appropriate play
- Pacifier or other appropriate soothing methods
- Bathing and clothing changes
- Complementary feeding at appropriate developmental stages
- Toddler routines and interactions

Care events should have variable durations and outcomes.

Some actions should be ineffective if they do not address the baby's actual need.

The app should provide appropriate feedback without becoming punitive or overly complicated.

---

## 9. Audio and Notifications

Audio is central to immersion because the user is interacting with a physical doll rather than an animated baby on the screen.

Desired audio categories:

- Light fussing
- Moderate crying
- Intense crying
- Coos
- Babbling
- Giggles
- Burping
- Sleep-related sounds
- Settling and content sounds

Sounds must match developmental age and behavioral state.

We should use properly licensed or original audio assets.

### Notification Requirements

The app must continue simulating when:

- The screen is locked
- The user switches applications
- The phone is idle
- The app is not in the foreground

Android background execution, notification permissions, battery management, and audio restrictions must be addressed early in development.

The app should restore simulation state after ordinary process termination or device restart where supported.

Do not assume Android allows unrestricted continuous background execution. Investigate appropriate scheduling, persistence, and foreground-service requirements.

Users should have control over notification volume, vibration, and optional quiet-time behavior.

## 10. User Interface and Visual Design

**The app must look modern and professional, not like a children's video game.**

Avoid:

- Cartoon baby characters
- Bright arcade-style graphics
- Game currencies and points
- Excessive animations
- Cluttered screens
- Childish fonts
- Complicated navigation

Preferred direction:

- Modern Android Material 3 design principles
- Soft, calming colors
- Clean typography
- Rounded cards and controls
- Spacious layouts
- Simple navigation
- Accessible contrast
- Light and dark themes
- Subtle animations and status transitions

### Main Dashboard

The home screen should prominently display:

- Baby's name
- Selected age
- Current status (sleeping, awake, fussing, crying)
- Simulation running/paused status
- Current care request, if applicable
- Clear actions for responding to care needs
- Recent activity

The user should be able to understand what is happening immediately.

Do not turn the interface into a medical monitoring dashboard.

### Suggested Navigation

**Home:** Baby's current condition and interactions

**History:** Timeline of care, sleep, and activities

**Baby Profile:** Name, age, temperament, and preferences

**Settings:** Audio, notifications, simulation speed, and app preferences

Navigation should be minimal.

The user should be able to complete most care interactions with one or two taps.

## 11. Simulation Modes

### Normal Care

The primary immersive experience.

- Runs in real time
- Age-specific care
- Temperament-specific behavior
- Randomized events
- Day and night activity
- Realistic sounds and notifications

### Training Mode

An optional educational experience.

- Explains possible baby cues
- Offers guidance about appropriate care
- Helps users understand developmental behavior
- Provides context for caregiver decisions

Training Mode should not be required to enjoy the main app.

### Accelerated Mode

Allows shorter simulations and faster testing.

Useful for:

- Demonstrations
- Users who prefer shorter sessions
- Internal development and QA
- Testing 24-hour behavior distributions

The app should support pausing and resuming simulations.

## 12. Care History

Include a simple activity timeline.

Record:

- Feeding events
- Diaper changes
- Sleep periods
- Crying and fussing episodes
- Soothing attempts
- Play activities
- Missed or delayed care requests, where applicable

The history should help users understand the baby's daily routine.

Do not require account registration or cloud services for the initial version.

Store simulation state locally and preserve it between sessions.

## 13. Paid Expansion: Two Babies

**This is the second major development stage after the initial single-baby app is released and working reliably.**

Users have requested multiple baby simulations in the competitor's reviews.

We want to offer a paid expansion that allows **two babies to run simultaneously**.

### Expansion Features

- Two independently configured baby profiles
- Different ages
- Different temperaments
- Independent care schedules
- Independent sleep and feeding states
- Separate activity histories
- Distinguishable notifications and audio cues
- Ability to switch quickly between babies
- Simultaneous care requests
- A clear way to identify which baby needs attention

The two babies should not share the same schedule.

Their needs may overlap naturally.

The underlying architecture should support multiple independent simulation instances from the beginning, even though the initial release will only expose one baby.

This avoids rewriting the behavior engine when the expansion is introduced.

### Monetization

The base application will be a one-time purchase.

The second-baby capability will be an optional one-time in-app purchase.

Pricing will be determined after market testing.

Avoid subscriptions for the initial product.

## 14. Platform Strategy

### Phase One: Android

Build, test, and release on Google Play.

Primary goals:

- Reliable simulation engine
- Good Android background operation
- Modern interface
- Stable notifications and audio
- Positive user feedback
- Initial paid sales

### Phase Two: Paid Two-Baby Expansion

Release after the single-baby app has been validated.

### Phase Three: Apple iOS

Once Android sales and user feedback justify further investment, release an iPhone version.

We should evaluate a cross-platform framework early, but not sacrifice Android reliability to support a platform we are not yet releasing on.

Possible options include Flutter, Kotlin with Jetpack Compose, or another well-supported approach.

Technology selection should account for background scheduling, local notifications, audio, persistence, and future iOS support.

## 15. Required Development Order

We want to develop this incrementally, testing each component before adding complexity.

### Step 1 — Requirements and Research

- Review BabySitMe functionality and user complaints.
- Study RealCare Baby schedules and behavioral features.
- Research age-specific infant behavior.
- Define baby states, care events, and temperament profiles.
- Identify realistic frequency and duration ranges.
- Document Android background-execution requirements.
- Define the MVP boundary.

**Deliverable:** Functional requirements and behavior model specification.

### Step 2 — Behavior Engine

Build the simulation logic independently of the visual interface.

- Baby state model
- Age profiles
- Temperament profiles
- Care event scheduling
- Controlled randomness
- Sleep and wake cycles
- Care response logic
- Persistent state
- Reproducible simulation tests

Test accelerated 24-hour and multi-day simulations.

Verify that behavior is varied, plausible, and not repetitive.

**Deliverable:** Working, testable behavior engine.

### Step 3 — Baby Profile and Configuration

Build:

- Baby naming
- Age selection
- Temperament selection
- Profile storage
- Simulation configuration

The user must be able to change age without losing their entire profile.

**Deliverable:** Working baby configuration system.

### Step 4 — Core Care Interactions

Implement:

- Feeding
- Burping
- Diaper changing
- Rocking
- Comforting
- Sleep-related actions
- Basic play

Connect actions to the behavior engine so they affect future events.

**Deliverable:** Functional interactive baby simulation.

### Step 5 — Audio and Notifications

Implement:

- Crying sounds
- Fussing
- Content vocalizations
- Care alerts
- Background scheduling
- Notification controls
- App restart recovery

Test on actual Android devices, including locked-screen and battery-saving conditions.

**Deliverable:** Reliable immersive experience outside the foreground app.

### Step 6 — Modern User Interface

Build and refine:

- Home dashboard
- Current care request
- Care action controls
- Baby profile screens
- Activity history
- Settings
- Dark and light themes

Use a consistent modern design system.

**Deliverable:** Polished, usable Android application.

### Step 7 — Simulation Modes and History

Add:

- Normal Care
- Accelerated Mode
- Optional Training Mode
- Activity timeline
- Pause and resume
- Additional settings

**Deliverable:** Feature-complete initial release candidate.

### Step 8 — Testing and Refinement

Test:

- Different ages
- Different temperaments
- Randomness and event frequency
- Full 24-hour simulations
- Multiple consecutive days
- Audio and notification reliability
- Battery usage
- Android compatibility
- State recovery
- User experience

Have my wife review behavior realism.

Conduct beta testing with reborn doll owners.

Fix bugs and adjust event frequencies before launch.

**Deliverable:** Stable Android MVP.

### Step 9 — Google Play Release

- Finalize app name and branding
- Prepare Google Play listing
- Create screenshots
- Set pricing
- Complete privacy and store requirements
- Release Android application
- Collect user feedback

**Deliverable:** Paid Android app available to customers.

### Step 10 — Paid Two-Baby Expansion

- Enable second baby
- Independent simulation instances
- Multiple-baby dashboard
- Separate notifications and histories
- Overlapping care events
- Google Play in-app purchase
- Expansion testing

**Deliverable:** Optional paid two-baby feature.

### Step 11 — Future Development

After sales and user feedback:

- Additional toddler behaviors
- More advanced interactions
- Additional audio
- More age profiles and personalization
- Other justified paid expansions
- Apple iOS release

Do not build these features before the core app is validated.

## 16. Architecture Requirements

The app should be modular and maintainable.

Suggested logical components:

**Simulation Engine**
- Internal state
- Behavior calculations
- Event generation
- Temperament modifiers
- Age-specific rules

**Care Interaction System**
- User actions
- Action validation
- State changes
- Care completion

**Scheduling System**
- Simulation time
- Random event timing
- Pause/resume
- Background scheduling
- Restart recovery

**Audio and Notification System**
- Sound selection
- Alerts
- Volume and preferences
- Background notification delivery

**Data Storage**
- Baby profiles
- Simulation state
- Care history
- App settings
- Purchase entitlements

**Presentation Layer**
- Dashboard
- Care controls
- History
- Profile management
- Settings

The simulation engine should not depend on UI components.

Design it to support multiple independent baby instances later.

Prefer local-first functionality, minimal infrastructure, and no recurring server expenses.

Do not add AI APIs or cloud dependencies unless a clearly justified feature requires them.

## 18. Project Constraints

- Keep initial development manageable.
- Avoid unnecessary features and complexity.
- No custom electronic doll hardware.
- No 3D baby rendering.
- No game-like appearance.
- No unnecessary AI subscriptions.
- No cloud backend unless required.
- Android first.
- One baby first.
- Two-baby expansion second.
- iOS only after commercial validation.
- Validate behavior before spending significant time polishing visuals.
- Use childcare expertise to guide realism.
- Make the experience enjoyable without making it unrealistically demanding.
- Use original or properly licensed assets and behavior content.

---

## 19. Success Criteria

The first release should be considered successful when:

1. A user can create and configure a baby with a selected age and temperament.
2. The baby behaves differently across developmental stages.
3. Care requests occur with realistic variability.
4. The user experiences meaningful activity throughout the day.
5. The baby responds appropriately to care interactions.
6. Sounds and notifications work reliably.
7. The simulation continues correctly when the phone is locked or the app is backgrounded, within supported Android constraints.
8. The user can review recent activity.
9. The app looks modern, polished, and professional.
10. Beta testers find it more immersive and interactive than BabySitMe.
11. The app is stable enough for paid distribution.
12. The architecture can support the planned second-baby expansion.

## 20. Instructions for Claude

Use this document as the project requirements baseline.

**Do not immediately generate the entire application.**

Begin by:

1. Reviewing the requirements and reference links.
2. Identifying any important missing functional requirements.
3. Recommending the best Android-first technology stack, considering a future iOS version.
4. Designing the behavior engine and data model.
5. Defining the event scheduling and randomness strategy.
6. Proposing a practical, testable MVP architecture.
7. Breaking the work into small development milestones following the build order above.

Prioritize the behavior engine before the interface.

The biggest differentiator is realistic baby behavior, not visual complexity.

Keep the application lightweight, immersive, reliable, and affordable to develop and maintain.

**The final experience should feel like having a real baby or toddler alongside the user's physical reborn doll—not like playing a baby-themed mobile game.**
Additional Requirement: Realistic Baby Audio Library
The application must use authentic, natural-sounding infant and toddler audio rather than synthetic game-style sound effects.
Research and source commercially licensed recordings from Freesound, Pixabay, ZapSplat, or other appropriate libraries.
Prioritize CC0 recordings when available.
Audio must be categorized by:

* Developmental age
* Vocalization type
* Intensity
* Duration
* Suitable simulation state

The behavior engine must control sound selection, escalation, repetition, and stopping when care is successfully provided.
